What these numbers mean

All of these are power ratios in decibels. +10 dB is 10× the power and +20 dB is 100×. Each bit of digital audio is worth about 6 dB.

SNR: signal-to-noise ratio

Signal power ÷ noise power. Noise is unrelated to the signal (hiss, hum, static) and is still there in the silences.

(S+N)/N

SNR with the signal measured noise-included, the way AM airband and HF receiver sensitivity is quoted. It is close to SNR when SNR is high, but at threshold 6 dB (S+N)/N is only 4.7 dB SNR.

THD: total harmonic distortion

Distortion is the signal itself bent out of shape (clipping, quantisation), so it comes and goes with the signal. Like a real receiver spec, it is set and quoted as THD on a standard 1 kHz test tone, measured within the audio band: harmonics the audio filter removes don't count. In dBc (relative to the tone): −20 dBc = 10%, −40 dBc = 1%.

The distortion is then a fixed property of the "radio", applied unchanged to whatever you play. SDR (this clip) is the distortion actually measured on the current sound, and differs from the tone figure because speech and music don't distort the way a steady tone does.

SINAD: signal-to-noise-and-distortion

SINAD = (S + N + D) / (N + D). It counts everything unwanted, so it is never better than the worse of SNR and SDR. Radio receivers quote sensitivity as the input level giving 12 dB SINAD. For converters, effective bits ≈ (SINAD − 1.76) / 6.02.

Same number, different sound

SINAD says how much is wrong, not what kind. Try “Noise only (SNR 20 dB)” and “Distortion only (10% THD)”: on the test tone both give 20 dB SINAD, but noise is a hiss you can listen past, while distortion makes the sound harsh.

Reading the plots

Grey (original) is the clean source. Blue (degraded) is what you hear: the source plus everything added. Red (added) is just the noise + distortion, blue minus grey, which is what SINAD measures. In the spectrum, noise is a broad floor and distortion appears as new peaks at multiples of the signal's frequencies (clearest with the 1 kHz tone). The FFT floor sits below the SNR figure because the noise is spread over about 2000 frequency bins.

How it's measured

All figures are in-band: the reference is the original passed through the same audio filter, so a narrow bandwidth is not counted as distortion (frequency response is a separate spec), and noise is filtered too. Noise is scaled to hit the SNR exactly. The distortion strength is searched until the test tone's THD matches. SINAD is measured on the final output. Signal power is averaged over the whole clip, pauses included.

Radio presets

Each uses the figure from the named standard: the sensitivity threshold, or the strong-signal limits. Where a spec gives (S+N)/N, it is converted to SNR. ETSI SINAD figures are psophometrically weighted and are used as-is (about 2.5 dB kinder than unweighted). Real FM near threshold also has clicks, and HF has static crashes, which plain white noise doesn't capture.
